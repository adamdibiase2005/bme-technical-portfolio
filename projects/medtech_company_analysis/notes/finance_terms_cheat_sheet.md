## Revenue

What it means: money a company earns

Formula: price x quantity sold= revenue

Why investors care:
Revenue shows how big a company is.
Change in revenue --> demonstrates company's growth


## Gross Profit

Gross profit is revenue minus the direct cost of producing or delivering the product or service.

Formula: Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold = Gross Profit

Why it matters:
Gross profit shows how much money is left after direct production costs.


## Gross Margin

What it means: it's % profit

Formula:
gross profit / revenue = gross margin

Why investors care:
Shows the capital distribution between operating expenses and profit.


## Operating Income

What it means: 
Operating income is the profit left after subtracting operating expenses
like research and development, sales and marketing, and administrative costs.

formula:
gross profit - operating expenses = operating income

Why investors care:
Shows whether a core business is profitable before interest & taxes


## Operating Margin

What it means:
Operating margin shows operating income as a percentage of revenue.

Formula:
operating income / revenue

Why investors care:
It helps compare profitability between companies of different sizes.


## Net Income

What it means:
Net income is the company’s final profit after all expenses, interest, taxes, and other items.

Formula:
Operating income - interest +/- other gains/losses = net income

Why investors care:
It is often called the “bottom line,” but it does not always equal cash generated by the business.


## EBITDA

EBITDA means Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization

Why it matters:
Investors use EBITDA to compare operating performance between companies before financing and accounting differences.

Simple warning:
EBITDA is useful, but it is not the same thing as cash flow.


## Depreciation and Amortization 

The difference:
Depreciation is physical, ex: car depreciates in value (it's worth less every yr).

Amortization is the depreciation of non-physical assets,
ex: the cost of a 10yr patent is considered as an expense of 10% of its cost for 10 years straight.


## Operating Cash Flow

What it means: cash generated from operations

Why investors care:
Answers: is the company actually producing cash?


## Capex (Capital Expenditures)

What it means: 
The money a company spends on big long term investments that will benefit them for years (medtech ex: R&D lab or machinery)

Why investors care: 
It shows how much a company must reinvest to sustain or grow. High capex means growth but reduces free cash flow


## Free Cash Flow

What it means: 
Free cash flow is cash flow from operations minus capital expenditures.

Formula:
Cash Flow from Operations - Capital Expenditures = Free Cash Flow

Why it matters:
Free cash flow shows how much cash is left after reinvesting in the business.


## Margin

A margin expresses profit as a percentage of revenue.

Examples:

Gross margin  
Operating margin  
Net margin  
Free cash flow margin

Why it matters:
Margins help compare companies even when they are different sizes.


## Balance Sheet

The balance sheet shows what a company owns, what it owes, and the value left for shareholders.

Basic structure:

Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders’ Equity

Why it matters:
It shows the company’s financial position at a specific point in time.

## Income Statement

The income statement shows revenue, expenses, and profit over a period of time.

Why it matters:
It helps analyze growth and profitability.


## Cash Flow Statement

The cash flow statement shows how cash moves in and out of the company.

Main sections:

Operating cash flow  
Investing cash flow  
Financing cash flow

Why it matters:
It helps show whether the company is actually generating cash.
